# Decision: The VP's first team is the leadership team

## Context

This builds on [The director runs an organization through senior managers and quarterly bets](director-shape.md), which made the third career level a game of headcount, strategy bets and presenting upward, played through senior managers, with the player's executive and peer directors as characters. That decision kept executive politics as one strand and left it for a level above to make central. The HLD's *Director and above* spans several career levels; this decision shapes the VP, the fourth and top level, whose promotion ends the run as completed.

The HLD's success metrics ask that each promotion feel like a new job. The VP's risk is the same as the director's was, one level up: reading as the director with a bigger organization.

## Decision Elements

- **A new job** — *major* (tenet: *A fresh role over a continuous system*). Whether a promoted director can name things they no longer do and things they now have to learn.
- **People first** — *major* (tenet: *Show it through people over through numbers*). Whether the level is still played through characters and their reactions.
- **Engagement** — *major* (tenet: *Fun over authenticity in pace and process*). Whether a quarter still plays as scenes with reactions, not as a planning screen.
- **Fidelity to the job** — *moderate*. Whether what an engineering VP does is present: sharing a budget with the other leaders, being held to the company's priorities, communicating with a whole function, and making cuts.
- **Learnability** — *moderate* (tenet: *Learnable over ambiguous*). Whether better choices can be designed and seen to pay off.
- **Build cost** — *moderate*. How much is new rather than reused from the director level and the simulation core.

## Options in the Domain

### The leadership team and the function

The player is one of a leadership team: the CEO, who is their manager, and two peer VPs, each a full character with trust in the player. Below them are three directors, each leading a division seen only as an aggregate. The level's main levers are new: annual planning, where the function's headcount for the year is argued for against the peers' needs from one company budget; the company's priorities, handed down by the CEO each half, which the function is held to; an all-hands each quarter, where the player tells the whole function how things stand; cuts, when the company asks for them, which can be done once and honestly or spread thin; and disputes inside the leadership team. The habits become how closely the player directs their directors and which team the player puts first: their function, or the leadership team.

- **A new job — strong.** Placing heads, funding single bets and presenting the quarter to one executive are retired into the directors' hands or replaced; sharing a budget with peers, carrying someone else's priority, speaking to a whole function and making cuts are new.
- **People first — strong.** The CEO, the peers and the directors are the characters; divisions stay numbers, which is what a VP sees of them.
- **Engagement — strong.** The moments loop, recap and habits carry over as a frame filled with new content.
- **Fidelity to the job — strong.** Planning, priorities, communication and cuts are each a moment; culture and belonging fit as situations and goals.
- **Learnability — strong.** Asking for what the plan needs, putting the leadership team first, telling the function the truth and making one clean cut are each a clearly better choice that sound practice teaches.
- **Build cost — partial.** Reuses the character system, the aggregates, the engagement survey and the director level's folding of teams; needs the leadership team's moments, annual planning, priorities, cuts, the all-hands, a new level module, renderer and content.

*Summary:* the new job the HLD asks for, with executive politics finally central and still played through people, at a moderate build cost.

### The board and the market

The player is a CEO-in-waiting: board meetings, the company's numbers, competitors and the market are the core activity, and the engineering function appears as a few capacity numbers.

- **A new job — strong.** Facing a board and a market is plainly new.
- **People first — weak.** Board members are thin characters, and the people the player runs fade into numbers.
- **Engagement — partial.** Board scenes play as moments, but market events read as a business sim.
- **Fidelity to the job — weak.** It is a CEO's job, not an engineering VP's; the HLD's game is engineering management.
- **Learnability — weak.** Market outcomes resist clearly better choices.
- **Build cost — weak.** A market model and company finances, with little reuse.

*Summary:* a new job, but the wrong one, and it leaves the game's subject behind.

### The organization in crisis

The level is driven by large events: a reorganization, an acquisition to absorb, cuts, an outage that reaches the press. Each quarter brings one, and the player steers the function through it.

- **A new job — partial.** The events are new; between them the level is the director's with more people.
- **People first — strong.** Each crisis is a story about people.
- **Engagement — strong.** Events make vivid quarters.
- **Fidelity to the job — partial.** Crises are part of the job, but a VP's ordinary work (planning, priorities, peers) disappears.
- **Learnability — partial.** Each crisis can have a better path, but the level has no steady lever to learn.
- **Build cost — partial.** Each crisis is its own mechanic.

*Summary:* vivid, but a string of events rather than a job, and the ordinary work it leaves out is where a VP's skill lies.

## Selection

**The leadership team and the function.** It is the only option strong on all three major criteria. *Show it through people* rules out the board, which lets both the function and the leadership team fade into numbers, and the HLD's subject, engineering management, rules it out again. The organization in crisis keeps its best material as one strand: cuts are a moment of the chosen option, and the other crises fit as situations.

Its risk, reading as the director with a bigger organization, is guarded by three commitments: the director's levers (placing heads, funding bets, the quarterly review to one executive) are retired; the level's main levers face sideways (the peers and the shared budget) and down to the whole function (the all-hands, cuts), which no earlier level has; and the habit the level turns on asks which team the player belongs to first.

Committing obligates:
- divisions, each an aggregate team led by a director, using the director level's folding at the handover;
- the CEO and two peer VPs as people outside the organization;
- annual planning, company priorities, cuts, the all-hands and leadership disputes as moments;
- a VP level module, with its habits, moments, review measures and goals;
- a handover that converts a promoted director run: the player's groups fold into one division with a director, and two more divisions join;
- a renderer for the level's panels and moments;
- VP situation content, written about directors, divisions, the CEO and the peers;
- balance strategies for the new level, and the playtests' scripted players at it;
- promotion past the VP ending the run as completed, the top of the ladder.

It forecloses acting on groups, teams or anyone inside a division at the VP level, except through situations a director brings.
